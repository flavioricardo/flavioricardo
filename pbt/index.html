Our Outdoor FactoryDelivering Diverse Revenue Streams

Our mineral and royalty interests represent perpetual real property ownership of the underlying hydrocarbon and mineral resources. We lease development rights to operators and in turn, are entitled to a share of resource production unburdened by operating costs and capital expenditures.

Similarly, our surface ownership entitles us to many diverse, generally cost-free income streams related to oil and gas and other industrial infrastructure development on our land:

Surface use royalties

  • Diversified, fee-based, recurring revenues
  • Most governed through surface use agreements, easements, etc.

Other surface and land resources revenue

  • Contractual arrangements for sand extraction
  • Revenue from freshwater sales, leasing, rental, ranching and digital infrastructure uses

The PBT team pursues new, non-correlated revenue streams across energy and infrastructure sectors. Growth in AI data centers and in wind, solar, and natural gas generation is driving demand for land, reliable power, and water, all of which our land can provide.

The Permian Basin

Location is the differentiator.

Every land and minerals company in West Texas earns the same two things: surface royalties and mineral royalties. What sets them apart is the ground they hold, and who operates on it.

Map: PBT Land & Minerals, November 2026.

Where the crowd is

Delaware and Midland.

The two sub-basins produce the vast majority of Permian oil and gas today, and that is where most royalty companies compete for the same acreage.

Where PBT is

The Central Basin Platform.

Between the two, where Permian production began a century ago. Less crowded, and the natural disposal point as Delaware and Midland run out of pore space.

Crane County

One contiguous position.

On the Central Basin Platform, across Ward and Crane counties: one block of ground, not scattered parcels.
